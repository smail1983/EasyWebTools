How to Convert Text Case: Uppercase, Lowercase, Title Case and Sentence Case

By Smail Albadraoui · Published November 25, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Learn when to use uppercase, lowercase, title case and sentence case, with practical examples and a free text case converter.

What text case means

Text case describes how letters are capitalized in a word, sentence or heading. The same phrase can look formal, conversational, technical or attention-grabbing depending on its capitalization. Uppercase uses capital letters throughout, lowercase uses small letters, sentence case capitalizes the beginning of a sentence, and title case capitalizes important word starts. These styles are not interchangeable in every context. A heading, a legal name, a software identifier and a paragraph may each follow different conventions, so the best case depends on the audience and purpose.

When to use uppercase

Uppercase is useful for short labels, acronyms, compact interface controls and emphasis where a design system calls for it. It can make a small label stand out, but long passages in all caps are harder for many readers to scan because the word shapes become less distinctive. Avoid converting a person's name, a product name or a technical identifier without checking its official spelling. Acronyms such as HTML and URL are normally kept uppercase, even when the surrounding text uses sentence case.

When lowercase is appropriate

Lowercase is common in usernames, email addresses, code examples, search terms and informal text. Converting a pasted block to lowercase can remove accidental capitalization, but it can also change meaning. Product names, company names, place names and abbreviations may have intentional uppercase letters. In programming, case can be significant: a variable named userName may not be the same as username. Use lowercase conversion for ordinary prose only when you are comfortable reviewing these exceptions.

Title case and headline style

Title case is often used for article headlines, book titles, presentation headings and marketing labels. However, style guides differ about which short words remain lowercase. Some capitalize every word; others capitalize nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs while leaving short prepositions and articles lowercase unless they appear at the beginning or end. A simple converter applies a mechanical rule, not an editorial style guide. Treat its output as a starting point and review it against the publication's preferred style.

Sentence case for readable headings

Sentence case capitalizes the first letter of a sentence and leaves most other words in their normal form. It often works well for web headings, navigation labels and interface text because it feels direct and readable. When a heading contains a colon, question mark or abbreviation, a simple automatic converter may not know whether the next word should be capitalized. Check sentence boundaries, initials, acronyms and proper nouns after converting.

Examples from everyday work

A writer who receives a heading typed as HOW TO RESIZE AN IMAGE can convert it to sentence case for a calmer web style. A spreadsheet of category labels can be converted to title case for a consistent presentation, then reviewed for brand names. A social media editor can turn a paragraph into lowercase for a stylistic draft, but should restore proper nouns before posting. A developer can convert ordinary sample text, but should not blindly change case-sensitive code, URLs or identifiers.

Accessibility and consistency

Consistent capitalization helps people recognize the structure of a page. Avoid using all caps as the only way to communicate importance; combine clear wording, semantic headings and visual hierarchy. Keep button labels short and use the same case style across equivalent controls. If a site uses sentence case in its navigation, mixing title case and uppercase in neighboring items can make the interface feel inconsistent. A team style guide can record preferred treatment for headings, buttons, acronyms and product names.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is trusting automatic conversion without proofreading. A simple title-case rule may capitalize small connecting words that your style guide keeps lowercase. Sentence-case conversion may miss sentence boundaries after abbreviations. Lowercase conversion may alter a brand name or code token. Another mistake is using capitalization to compensate for unclear writing. If a heading is difficult to understand, rewrite it before choosing its case. Clear wording is more important than a particular capitalization pattern.
