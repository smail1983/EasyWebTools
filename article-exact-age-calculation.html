How to Calculate Age Exactly in Years, Months and Days

Learn how exact age is calculated from a date of birth, including leap years, month lengths and common date pitfalls.

What exact age means

An exact calendar age expresses the time between a date of birth and a reference date in years, months and days. It is different from estimating age by dividing a number of elapsed days by 365.25. Calendar years can contain 365 or 366 days, and months vary from 28 to 31 days. A calendar-based calculation therefore needs to account for month boundaries and leap years. The answer also depends on the reference date and, in some cases, the local time zone or legal definition.

Start with two dates

To calculate age, you need a valid date of birth and a reference date. For a current age, the reference date is usually today. First subtract the birth year from the reference year. Then compare the month and day. If the birthday has not occurred yet in the reference year, subtract one from the year difference. The remaining months and days require borrowing from the previous calendar month when the day number is smaller than the birth day.

Why month lengths matter

A month is not a fixed number of days. If a date calculation needs to borrow days, it must use the length of the month immediately before the reference date. For example, subtracting a day value across a month boundary in March may involve February, which can have 28 or 29 days. A correct calendar calculation uses the actual calendar rather than assuming every month has 30 days.

Leap years and February birthdays

A leap year adds February 29 to the calendar. This matters when counting elapsed days and when handling a February 29 birthday in a non-leap year. Different legal systems may define when a person born on February 29 reaches an age threshold in a non-leap year. A general age calculator can show a calendar difference, but it cannot decide every legal convention. For official eligibility, follow the jurisdiction's rules and the authority's specified reference date.

Examples of calendar age

If someone was born on May 10 and the reference date is May 9 of a later year, the birthday has not yet occurred in that year, so the year count is reduced by one. If the reference date is June 12, the birthday has passed and the calculation can include the additional month and days. When the reference date falls early in a month and the birth day is later, the calculation borrows days from the previous month, whose length must be checked.

Age in days versus age in years

Some forms ask for a person's age in completed years, while other calculations need the exact elapsed number of days. These are different measures. Dividing days by 365 can produce a slightly different result from calendar age because leap days and different year lengths matter. For medical studies, contracts, insurance or legal deadlines, use the exact definition specified by the organization collecting the information.

Time zones and date-only values

A date of birth is often stored as a calendar date rather than a moment in time. JavaScript Date objects can introduce time-zone differences if a date-only string is parsed and then displayed in a different local zone. Around midnight, the local calendar date may differ from UTC. The EasyWebTools calculator uses the browser's current local date for the comparison. If you need a legal or international calculation, establish the relevant time zone and reference date explicitly.

Common mistakes

Do not assume every year has 365 days, every month has 30 days or a birthday has already occurred this year. Do not enter a future date as a birth date. Check whether the form asks for completed years, calendar years and months, or total days. Another common mistake is relying on a device with an incorrect date. A precise result is only as reliable as the dates and rules used to calculate it.

When an age calculator is appropriate

If a deadline or age threshold has legal consequences, confirm the applicable rule with the relevant authority. Frequently asked questions

Does exact age include leap years?

Calendar calculations account for leap-year dates, but legal treatment of a February 29 birthday can vary by jurisdiction.

Why is age not just days divided by 365?

Calendar years have different lengths and months vary, so a calendar age calculation uses year, month and day boundaries.

Which date does the calculator use?

Can I use it for legal eligibility?

Use the rule and reference date specified by the relevant authority; a general calculator is not a legal determination.
